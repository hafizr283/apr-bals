  1. Experiments and restructure (do these first)
        Build the missing control: an FP32 CUDA-core kernel with the WMMA dataflow. Take
        the dense WMMA kernel, keep everything (warp per entity, 16-row chunks staged in
        shared memory, FP32 register accumulators, upper triangle only, no atomics) and
        replace the mma_sync with a register-tiled 16×16×16 FMA loop. Run it in the Table 6
        ablation as a fourth variant. Right now the "kernel factor" bundles the tensor pipe with
        the dataflow, and since TF32 (half the FP16 rate, about the FP32 CUDA-core peak)
        already captures 87.8–99.6% of the gain and the dense kernel sits at 20.5% of the
        tensor pipe, a reviewer will assume the dataflow is the whole story. We need to know
        either way, and this also becomes the honest FP32 baseline for the two-path harness,
        replacing the 2%-of-peak atomic-flush scalar kernel as the reference.
        Carve out a validation split and re-select λ on it. λ = 0.048 was chosen on the test
        ratings. That is not acceptable for a paper whose accuracy claim is a 0.005% RMSE
        delta. Use 70/10/20 or 80/10/10, redo the §5.1 sweep on the validation set, and report
        test RMSE once. The relative comparison will not move, but none of the absolute
        numbers or the U-curve argument can be cited until this is done.
        Explain why APR-BALS has lower train and test RMSE at all 20 points. Do not leave
        "we have not isolated its cause" in the paper. My suspicion: the scalar baseline
        accumulates each Gram entry through FP32 atomic adds across tiles, so for the
        heaviest item (187,512 ratings) the accumulation error is bounded by γ^(32)_s with s ≈
        1.9×10⁵, about 10⁻², an order of magnitude larger than the FP16 operand-rounding term
        (≈ 10⁻³) that bounds the WMMA path. If that holds, the FP32 baseline is numerically
        worse than the FP16 path on heavy entities, which explains both the direction and the

  2. Rewrite these parts
        §1 contribution list. It is a results section in disguise: nearly a column of per-dataset
        speedups, RMSE deltas to three decimals, run-to-run variance, the ablation share, the
        26×/19.5× distinction, and the register-spill story. The reader has not seen the system
        yet and cannot interpret any of it, and it repeats the abstract, §4, §5 and the
        conclusion. One sentence per bullet stating what is new: density-adaptive dispatch to
        tensor cores; a blocked batched Cholesky for the solve stage; an in-binary ablation
        separating kernel design from precision; evaluation against an audited baseline and
        three external systems. At most one headline number. The baseline audit is a
        safeguard, not a contribution; it stays in §3.5.
        §1 motivation sentence on peak throughput. Remove "The RTX 3060 … delivers about
        25.5 TFLOP/s … twice its 12.7 TFLOP/s FP32 peak, and data-center parts widen this
        ratio considerably." It frames the paper as a throughput play that our own §4.5
        disproves, it promises bigger gains on A100/H100 that we never tested and that the T4
        result (4× larger ratio, smaller speedup) argues against, and device peaks belong in
        Table 3. Replace with the argument we can support: tensor cores give a register-
        resident, atomic-free accumulation dataflow that sparse Gram assembly can use once
        entities are grouped by density.
        §3.5, §3.6, §5.1, §5.3: stop narrating what went wrong first. "An early version of this
        kernel was defective," "crippled," "would have inflated the speedups to 30–44×," "a first
        warp-cooperative packed solver," "an early version of this work used a uniform
        penalty," "tried and discarded." Keep the content, change the register. §3.5 becomes a
        register-pressure analysis: a block owning a 32-row tile needs K²/32 persistent
        accumulators per thread, exceeding the 64-register budget at K ≥ 48, so each tile row
        is split over G sibling blocks; ptxas confirms no spills for any launched template. The
        one sentence worth keeping about the defect goes to the limitations subsection as a
        general caution: register-blocked FP32 Gram baselines that spill at high K understate
        FP32 throughput severalfold and inflate reported tensor-core speedups. In §3.6, §5.1
        and §5.3 state the chosen design and, in one clause, the alternative it was measured
        against.
        §2 opening sentence. Enumerate the four lines of research (i)–(iv) in the order of §2.1–
        2.4 so it works as a roadmap.
        §2 citation placement. Put the bracket right after the named author or system ("Koren

        …", "Hogwild! [19] …"), and when one sentence bundles several works attach each
